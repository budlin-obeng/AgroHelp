## 1. Geographic & Agro-Ecological Profile

- **District:** Ejura-Sekyedumase Municipal District
- **Region:** Ashanti Region, Ghana
- **Coordinates:** `7.3856° N, -1.3562° W`
- **Agro-Ecological Zone:** Forest-Savannah Transition Zone (Transition Zone IV)
- **Topography:** Gently undulating plains ranging between 150 m and 300 m above mean sea level
- **Soil Characteristics:** Predominantly sandy loam to loamy sand (Chromic and Ferric Luvisols, Savannah Ochrosols); characterized by low natural organic carbon ($< 1.2\%$), high permeability, rapid drainage, and high susceptibility to nutrient leaching and erosion under torrential rainfall.

---

## 2. Dominant Crops in the District

1. **Cereals (Baseline Cash Crops):** Maize (*Zea mays*), Rice (*Oryza sativa*), Sorghum (*Sorghum bicolor*).
2. **Legumes (Biological Nitrogen Fixers):** Cowpea (*Vigna unguiculata*), Groundnut (*Arachis hypogaea*), Soybean (*Glycine max*).
3. **Roots & Tubers (Soil Breakers & Drought Anchors):** White Yam (*Dioscorea rotundata*), Cassava (*Manihot esculenta*).
4. **Plantain & Horticultural Crops:** Plantain, garden eggs, okra, tomatoes, and hot peppers.

---

## 3. The Central Smallholder Challenge: 88% Maize Monoculture

> *"Currently, 88% of Ejura-Sekyedumase relies on rain-fed, continuous maize monoculture heavily dependent on synthetic fertilizers. By using NASA Earth observations to predict dry spells, our tool guides farmers to rotate in drought-tolerant cowpeas—disrupting the Fall Armyworm cycle naturally and reducing expensive fertilizer dependency."*  
> — **MOFA District Agronomic Assessment**

### Compounding Risks:
1. **Severe Fall Armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda*) Infestations:**
   - Continuous maize cultivation across the major and minor seasons creates an uninterrupted green canopy and food supply for armyworm larvae.
   - Pests transition effortlessly between adjacent plantings, rendering chemical control expensive (averaging GHS 450–800/ha per season) and largely ineffective.
2. **Severe Fertilizer Dependency & Cost Inflation:**
   - Continuous cereal cropping rapidly extracts 90–120 kg N/ha per season without natural replenishment.
   - Smallholders become trapped purchasing imported synthetic fertilizers (NPK 15:15:15 and Urea), which consume up to 45% of total production budgets.
3. **Erratic Rainfall & Early Season Termination:**
   - **Major Cropping Season (April – July):** Subject to delayed onset and false starts.
   - **Minor Cropping Season (September – November):** Increasingly abbreviated by early cessation of the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) and early arrival of the dry, dusty Harmattan wind.
4. **Destructive Windstorms & Soil Erosion:**
   - High rainfall erosivity ($MFI = 143.2$) strips bare topsoil following harvest, accelerating soil acidification and compaction.

---

## 4. Farmer Decision Priorities (Ranked Order)

When deciding on planting rotations, smallholders evaluate five clear priorities:

1. **Market Suitability & Cash Income (The #1 Priority):** Crop produce must have high local liquidity, immediate aggregation buyers, or established processing off-takers.
2. **Input Cost Reduction (Fertilizer & Pesticide Savings):** Lowering cash expenditure on synthetic nitrogen and toxic agro-chemicals.
3. **Pest Cycle Interruption (Fall Armyworm Suppression):** Planting non-host dicot crops that starve larvae and interrupt generational breeding cycles naturally.
4. **Water Use & Drought Resilience:** Crops capable of completing phenological development within shortened or erratic minor season windows.
5. **Labor & Equipment Compatibility:** Planting and harvesting demands must align with available family labor or low-cost tractor hire services.
